# Hook library

The governing rule is in SKILL.md: **withhold the subject, pose a real question, and
answer that exact question in the last line.** Everything here is a shape for doing that.

A hook fails if the viewer can name the subject from it, or if the ending does not settle
the debt it opened.

## Shapes

**The contradiction.** State two things that cannot both be true.
> "The most peaceful era in this nation's history ran entirely on hostages."

**The impossible number.** A figure the viewer will refuse to believe.
> "This country had more guns than all of Europe combined. Then it spent two hundred and
> fifty years pretending it never did."

**The wrong reaction.** Someone behaves in a way no sane person would.
> "The man in charge was woken up and told the city was burning. He said it was nothing
> and went back to bed."

**The deliberate handicap.** Someone gives up an obvious advantage on purpose.
> "He builds tools that are forbidden from reaching the internet. He wrote that rule
> himself."

**The missing thing.** Something famous is absent and nobody asks why.
> "Half of this building is missing, and it did not fall down."

**The inverted cost.** The cheap thing is precious, or the precious thing was traded away.
> "They won the war, and handed the island back anyway. For a spice."

**The unnoticed clock.** Time has been running on something the viewer never counted.
> "You have about twenty seven years of free time left. Most of it is already spoken for."

**The origin nobody claims.** A thing everyone uses that somebody specific built and
regrets.
> "The thing keeping you on your phone was invented by one person. He has spent years
> trying to undo it."

## Writing them

- Swap every proper noun for a placeholder: "this country", "the man in charge", "one
  developer", "a designer".
- Keep it to one or two sentences. A hook that needs three is a summary.
- Be specific about the *oddity* while staying vague about the *subject*. "More guns than
  all of Europe" is specific; "a surprising military fact" is not.
- Say the closing line out loud before you write the body. If it does not answer the hook,
  fix one of them now rather than after the film is cut.

## Anti-patterns

- **A tease with nothing behind it.** "You will not believe what this city did." No
  question, no oddity, nothing owed.
- **A reveal that is only a name.** If the payoff is "it was London", the viewer feels
  tricked. The name is not the answer; the *reason* is.
- **A hook the body abandons.** The middle exists to earn the answer. If beats two through
  eight have nothing to do with the question, the structure is wrong.
- **A proper noun smuggled in.** "In 1666, the man in charge..." gives away the era and
  half the subject.
